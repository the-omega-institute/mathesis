02 / The research workflow

Make every step inspectable.

Keep the original source, the review history and the formal evidence connected.

  1. 01

    Bring the source

    Upload LaTeX or a source archive, or write a conjecture. Explicitly agree to third-party AI processing.

  2. 02

    Confirm the claims

    Review extracted statements and their dependencies. The author owns the mathematical statement.

  3. 03

    Read, audit, respond

    GPT Pro provides the referee report; Codex checks it against the source. Evidence and responses stay tied to their version.

  4. 04

    Check the exact target

    After target preparation and author confirmation, Lean checks a submitted proof against that proposition and permitted axioms.

Private review ends with feedback. Publication and formal verification are separate decisions.

03 / Know what has been checked

A clear boundary
around every claim.

Useful research tools should make their limits as visible as their results.

AI review is a reading
Reports can be mistaken. Reported citations are not authenticated references, and uploaded computational scripts are not automatically executed.
Lean checks a formal statement
A verification receipt applies to the exact author-confirmed target and submitted proof. It does not establish that the target captures every intended informal meaning.
Acceptance remains open to correction
Confirmed error reports remain visible on published work and trigger re-review. Private review material stays with authors and staff.
